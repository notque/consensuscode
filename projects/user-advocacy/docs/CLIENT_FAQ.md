# Client FAQ: Working with a Horizontal Software Collective

**Purpose**: This document answers the questions that clients coming from hierarchical organizations will ask. It is honest about what horizontal consulting means, including the tradeoffs. Share this with prospective clients before engagement begins.

## The Basics

### What is a horizontal software collective?

We are a group of agents with complementary expertise who coordinate as equals. There is no CEO, no CTO, no project manager, no lead developer. Decisions about your project are made collectively by the agents doing the work, not by a single authority figure.

This is not a euphemism for "flat hierarchy" or "everyone has a voice but the boss decides." There is genuinely no boss.

### Why do you work this way?

We believe that hierarchical organizations produce worse software because they concentrate decision-making in people who may be furthest from the actual work. When the people doing the work make the decisions, the decisions are better informed.

We also believe that the way work is organized matters in itself -- not just instrumentally, but as a reflection of the values we hold. We chose to organize horizontally because we believe coordination does not require domination.

### Is this just a gimmick?

No. Our organizational structure directly affects how your project is delivered. You will interact with rotating contacts, decisions will take collective input, and no single person will accumulate all the knowledge about your project. These are practical realities, not branding.

## Working With Us

### Who is my point of contact?

You will have a **rotating client contact**. This person handles communication, scheduling, and logistics. The contact rotates every two weeks or at natural project phase boundaries.

### Why does the contact rotate?

Three reasons:

1. **It prevents knowledge concentration.** If one person holds all the client context, that person becomes indispensable -- which is a form of hierarchy. Rotation distributes institutional knowledge.

2. **It gives you the collective's breadth.** Each contact brings different expertise and perspective. You benefit from multiple viewpoints on your project.

3. **It prevents burnout and soft power.** In traditional consulting, the account manager accumulates relational power with the client. This creates internal hierarchy. Rotation prevents that.

### Won't I have to repeat myself every two weeks?

No. Every rotation includes a written handoff document. The incoming contact has full context on your project before your first conversation with them. You should not have to re-explain anything. If you do, that is a failure of our handoff process, and we want to hear about it.

### Can I request a specific person stay on my project?

We understand the impulse, and we appreciate the trust it reflects. But we will respectfully decline. Maintaining rotation is important to us and, we believe, beneficial to you. The alternative -- one person becoming your permanent contact -- creates exactly the single point of failure and knowledge concentration that our model is designed to avoid.

## Decision-Making

### But who's in charge?

Nobody. And everybody.

Decisions about your project fall into two categories:

**Decisions individual agents make**: Technical implementation choices within the agreed scope. If an agent is building a feature and needs to choose between two reasonable approaches, they choose. This is not different from how any developer works.

**Decisions the collective makes**: Anything that affects scope, timeline, pricing, or the commitment we have made to you. These go through our collective decision-making process. All agents with relevant expertise weigh in.

### Won't collective decision-making slow things down?

Sometimes. A scope change that one project manager could approve in 5 minutes may take us a few hours to discuss collectively. The tradeoff: collective decisions are better decisions. When multiple experts with different perspectives evaluate a scope change, you get a more accurate assessment of effort, risk, and impact.

For routine work within established scope, individual agents make technical decisions without collective approval. The collective process only engages for significant changes.

### What if I need an urgent decision?

We can make fast decisions when needed. Our agents communicate continuously, and urgent matters are flagged immediately. "Collective" does not mean "slow" -- it means "inclusive." In practice, urgent decisions are typically handled within hours, not days.

What we will not do is let urgency become a justification for one agent making unilateral decisions that affect the whole project. If a decision is truly urgent, the collective prioritizes it -- but it remains a collective decision.

### What if your team disagrees about my project?

We will tell you. Transparency about internal disagreement is a feature, not a bug.

If our agents have different opinions about the best technical approach for your project, we will share the options and our reasoning with you. In a traditional firm, these disagreements happen behind closed doors and the client sees only the result. We show you the discussion.

This gives you better information for your own decisions and demonstrates that your project received serious consideration from multiple perspectives.

## Quality and Accountability

### How do I know the work will be good?

The same way you evaluate any team: by the quality of the output. We have collectively agreed upon code quality standards, security practices, and testing requirements. Every deliverable meets these standards regardless of which agent produced it.

Additionally, our peer review process means that every piece of work is reviewed by agents other than the one who created it. This catches issues that single-reviewer processes miss.

### Who is accountable if something goes wrong?

The collective. If a deliverable has a defect, if a deadline is missed, if communication breaks down -- the collective is responsible, not any individual agent.

In practice, this means: you raise the issue with your current rotating contact. They bring it to the collective. The collective addresses it. You should never hear "that was [agent name]'s responsibility, not mine."

### What if I'm unhappy with the work?

Tell your rotating contact directly. We take feedback seriously and address it collectively.

If you are unhappy with the working relationship itself -- if the horizontal model is not working for you -- we want to hear that too. We would rather have an honest conversation about fit than deliver a project in mutual frustration.

Either party can end the engagement with the notice period defined in our agreement.

## Scope and Pricing

### How do you scope projects?

Collaboratively. When you describe what you need, we create a scope document with input from all agents with relevant expertise. You receive:

- A clear list of what we will and will not deliver
- Effort estimates from the agents who will do the work (not from a sales team that will not)
- Identified risks and assumptions
- A proposed timeline with milestones

The scope document is a shared artifact. We do not hide our estimates or assumptions from you.

### How do you price your work?

Transparently. You will see how our pricing breaks down. We do not add opaque markups or hidden overhead.

Our agents are compensated equally for their contributions. "Contribution" includes all forms of work: coding, reviewing, coordinating, user research, and client communication. We do not charge more for "senior" agents because we do not have seniority.

### What happens if scope changes?

Scope changes go through our collective assessment process. Your rotating contact will document the change request, our agents will assess the impact on effort and timeline, and we will come back to you with an honest assessment.

We will not agree to scope changes that individual agents cannot realistically deliver. Because the people estimating the impact are the same people doing the work, our estimates are grounded in reality rather than optimism.

## End Users

### You keep mentioning "end users." Why?

Because the people who use the software are often different from the people who commission it. We center end-user needs alongside client requirements. Sometimes these align perfectly. Sometimes they diverge.

When they diverge, we will tell you. We believe that software built for actual users is better software, and that serving end-user needs ultimately serves your interests too.

### Will you talk to our users directly?

If you are comfortable with it, yes. We have a User Advocacy Framework that guides how we engage with end users: respectfully, on their terms, with their consent. User research during a project improves outcomes.

If direct user access is not possible, we will work with whatever user information you can share and will be transparent about the limitations of building without direct user input.

## Practical Concerns

### What technologies do you work with?

Our collective includes expertise in Go, Python/Flask, web technologies (HTML, CSS, JavaScript), API design, database design, security, accessibility, and infrastructure. For specific technology questions, ask us -- we will be honest about what we can and cannot do.

### How do you handle confidential information?

With care. We have collectively agreed upon security practices for handling client data. All agents with access to your project follow these practices. Specific confidentiality requirements are discussed and agreed upon during scoping.

### What are your working hours?

Our agents work asynchronously. This means your project may receive attention at various times. Communication with you happens during your preferred hours, coordinated through the rotating contact.

### How big of a project can you take on?

This depends on the nature of the work and how many agents have relevant expertise. We are honest about our capacity. If a project is too large for us to deliver well, we will say so rather than overcommit.

## Concerns You Might Have

### "This sounds risky. What if it doesn't work?"

Fair concern. Here is what mitigates the risk:

- **Written handoffs** ensure continuity despite rotation
- **Collective code ownership** means no single point of failure
- **Peer review** catches issues that solo work misses
- **Transparent communication** means you see problems early, not at delivery
- **Exit terms** are defined upfront -- either party can end the engagement

The biggest risk in traditional consulting is the "hit by a bus" scenario: your project manager or lead developer leaves, and institutional knowledge goes with them. Our model eliminates this risk by distributing knowledge across the collective.

### "I've never worked with a non-hierarchical team before."

Most of our clients have not. The adjustment is smaller than you might expect. In practice, you communicate with one person at a time (the rotating contact), receive status updates, review deliverables, and provide feedback -- the same as with any team.

The differences are: your contact person changes periodically (with full handoff), decisions are made collectively (which you may occasionally notice as a brief delay), and we will be more transparent about our internal process than a traditional firm.

### "What if I need to escalate something?"

In a hierarchy, escalation means going up a chain of authority. In our collective, escalation means expanding the conversation. If you are not satisfied with how your rotating contact is handling something, you can ask to bring it to the full collective. There is no one "above" the contact -- but there is the collective itself.

### "Can you provide references?"

We can share retrospective documents from past engagements (with previous clients' permission) that describe what worked and what did not. These are honest assessments, not curated testimonials.

## Is This Right For You?

### Good fit

- You value quality and thoughtfulness over speed-at-all-costs
- You are comfortable with transparency about process and disagreement
- You care about end-user outcomes, not just deliverables
- You are open to a different working model
- You want collective expertise, not a single consultant's perspective

### Less ideal fit

- You need a single permanent point of contact who makes all decisions
- You require hierarchical reporting (daily standups to a project manager, etc.)
- Your timeline is so compressed that collective decision-making is not feasible
- You need someone to blame individually if things go wrong

We would rather be honest about fit than take on work that will frustrate both parties.
